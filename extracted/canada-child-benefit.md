## Start here

[paragraph] A monthly deposit from the government sounds like the setup to a scam call. This one is real, it's yours if you qualify, and the only trick is filing your taxes on time.

[paragraph] The Canada Child Benefit pays eligible families a tax-free amount every month for each child under 18. Preview how the CRA sizes your payment, meet the two income thresholds that shrink it, and learn the paperwork mistake that pauses even an eligible family. The goal is a file you can defend, not a guess you hope survives contact with the CRA.

[spotlight] The CCB is not added to your income and does not need to be reported as income on your tax return. It is calculated from your return, but it is not part of it.

[chips]

  - Catch the myths that stop eligible parents from ever applying.
  - Split a shared-custody claim correctly when a child has two homes.
  - Build a simple benefit file so a late or missing payment is easy to trace and fix.

[myths]
  - “The CCB counts as income, so I'll owe tax on it.” — It's tax-free. It never gets added to your income, and you never pay it back at tax time just for receiving it.

## How the CRA Calculates Your Payment

[paragraph] Four things move the number on your deposit. None of them require a calculator you don't already own.

[cards]
  - HOUSEHOLD INCOME — Your adjusted family net income (AFNI) combines your net income with your spouse or partner's. More income above the threshold shrinks the payment. It does not erase it.
  - AGES OF YOUR KIDS — Each child under 6 is worth more than a child aged 6 to 17. A birthday or a new baby changes your total mid-year, automatically.
  - YOUR TAX RETURN — CRA recalculates every July using the return you filed that spring. A late or missing return can pause your payment completely, no matter your income.
  - CUSTODY ARRANGEMENT — Full-time, primary, or shared custody changes who applies and how the amount gets divided between two homes.

[spotlight] For the July 2026–June 2027 benefit year, the maximum is $8,157 a year ($679.75 a month) for a child under 6, and $6,883 a year ($573.58 a month) for a child aged 6 to 17. These figures are indexed every July — check canada.ca for the current numbers.

[spotlight] Below $38,237 in AFNI, you get the full amount with no reduction. Between $38,237 and $82,847, the reduction percentage climbs with each child, from 7% for one child to 23% for four or more. Above $82,847, a fixed amount plus a smaller percentage applies to the extra income.

[myths]
  - “Earning any amount over the threshold cuts off the whole benefit.” — Income above the threshold shrinks the payment gradually. It almost never drops straight to zero unless household income is very high.

## Marcus and the Missing Half

[story] Meet Marcus
  detail: DEDICATED STORY
  ¶ Marcus Ferreira drove a delivery van in Hamilton and had his daughter Mia four nights out of every seven — real, close-to-equal parenting, not "visits." His goal that October was small and specific: buy Mia a pair of winter boots without a crack across the toe. Her current pair let in slush like they were built for wading.
  ¶ Marcus and Mia's mother, Priya, had split up eighteen months earlier. Priya had always handled "the government stuff," so when a coworker mentioned the Canada Child Benefit, Marcus laughed it off. "That goes to moms," he said. "Everybody knows that."
  ¶ His coworker, who shared custody of his own son, said that wasn't true. Marcus looked it up on his lunch break. The CRA page said the benefit went to whoever was primarily responsible for a child's care, and if two parents split time close to equally, each one could apply for half.
  ¶ He almost closed the laptop right there. He hadn't filed a tax return the year before: three months between jobs, next to no income, and he'd figured a return with nothing on it wasn't worth the trouble. The page in front of him said something inconvenient: no return, no benefit, income or not.
  ¶ He filed. Forty minutes and one annoyed phone call to his sister for an old T4 slip. He filled out the CRA's shared-custody form and checked his account two days later, expecting good news. It said his estimated payment was zero.
  ¶ His first instinct was that the whole thing was a government-shaped practical joke. His second instinct, the useful one, was to call and ask why. The agent explained his address on file was three moves out of date, which had flagged his shared-custody claim for a manual review instead of an automatic approval.
  ¶ He fixed the address. Nine days later, a payment landed — half of what Mia was worth on paper, backdated to cover the months the file had been stuck. He spent the first deposit on the boots. Purple, slightly ridiculous, completely waterproof. Mia wore them through every puddle on the walk to school, which is the universal sign of a satisfied customer.

[spotlight] Marcus's file was correct. It was his address that was wrong. Address mismatches are the most common reason a shared-custody claim stalls in review.

## Who Actually Qualifies

[paragraph] Four boxes, and most families tick all of them without realizing it.

[cards]
  - LIVE WITH THE CHILD — You live with the child, and they're under 18.
  - PRIMARY CARE — You're mainly responsible for their day-to-day care — meals, school, appointments, the whole job.
  - STATUS IN CANADA — You or your spouse or partner is a citizen, permanent resident, protected person, or a temporary resident who's lived here 18 months or more.
  - TAXES FILED, EVERY YEAR — You and your spouse or partner file a return annually, even in a year with zero income.

[spotlight] Shared custody is defined as the child living with you at least 40% of the time, or on an approximately equal basis between two addresses. Both caregivers apply, and both must report any custody change to the CRA right away.

[paragraph] New parents can apply through the Automated Benefits Application when they register a birth, instead of filing separate paperwork later. A child approved for the disability tax credit can also add the Child Disability Benefit on top of the regular payment, and most provinces fold their own child benefit into the same monthly deposit.

[myths]
  - “Only mothers can get the Canada Child Benefit.” — Whoever is primarily responsible for the child's day-to-day care can apply — any gender, any relationship to the child. Shared-custody parents can each get half.
  - “You need to be a Canadian citizen to receive it.” — Permanent residents, protected persons, and even some temporary residents who've lived in Canada 18 months or more can qualify. Citizenship isn't required.

## Quick Check: Choose the Best Next Move

[accordions]
  - Your family's AFNI is $30,000, with two kids ages 4 and 9. What happens to your payment? — Nothing shrinks. Full maximum for both kids. Your AFNI sits below the $38,237 no-reduction threshold.
  - Your child turns 6 partway through the benefit year. What changes? — The payment steps down to the 6-to-17 rate starting the month after the birthday. CRA adjusts it automatically — no form needed.
  - You and your ex split time with your daughter close to equally, but only your ex has ever applied. What should you do? — Apply as a shared-custody caregiver through CRA My Account or form RC66. Once approved, you start getting your half going forward.
  - You forgot to file last year's taxes. Does that cancel this year's CCB? — It pauses it. File your return, and your spouse or partner's if you have one, and CRA recalculates, often with a retroactive payment once it's processed.
  - Your one-child family's AFNI jumps from $70,000 to $95,000 this year. What happens next July? — Next July's payment drops, using the second-threshold formula on the higher income. This July's payment stays put. It's still based on last year's lower AFNI.

## Glossary of Terms

[glossary]
  - Canada Child Benefit (CCB) — A tax-free monthly payment from the Canada Revenue Agency that helps eligible families cover the cost of raising a child under 18.
  - Adjusted family net income (AFNI) — The income number the CRA uses to size your payment — your net income plus your spouse or common-law partner's, with a few deductions applied.
  - Benefit year — The 12-month stretch from July to June where your CCB amount stays fixed, based on the tax return you filed the spring before.
  - Shared custody — An arrangement where a child splits time close to equally between two homes that aren't the same household. Each caregiver can get half of what they'd receive with full-time custody.
  - Automated Benefits Application — A one-step option that lets new parents apply for the CCB while registering their baby's birth, instead of filing a separate form later.
  - Child Disability Benefit — An extra tax-free monthly amount added on top of the CCB for a child approved for the disability tax credit.
