## Start here

[paragraph] A dental chair is no place to discover that “covered” and “free” are distant cousins who only exchange holiday cards.

[paragraph] The Canadian Dental Care Plan, or CDCP, helps eligible Canadian residents who lack access to private dental coverage and have adjusted family net income below $90,000. It pays participating oral-health providers according to CDCP rules and established fees. A member may still owe a co-payment, a fee difference or the full cost of an uncovered service.

[spotlight] All four eligibility requirements matter: no access to private dental insurance, required Canadian tax returns filed, adjusted family net income below $90,000 and Canadian residence.

[chips]
  - Screen CDCP eligibility without self-approving.
  - Confirm the coverage period and provider participation.
  - Separate co-payment from additional fees.
  - Identify services needing preauthorization.
  - Complete a Dental Coverage and Cost Confirmation Sheet.

[myths]
  - “Declining available workplace dental insurance makes me eligible for CDCP.” — Access to private coverage can make a person ineligible even when they choose not to enrol or pay the premium. The plan checks access, not enthusiasm.

[source] Canada: CDCP eligibility
  url: https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/dental/dental-care-plan/eligibility.html

## Coverage Has Dates, Percentages and Rules

[paragraph] After approval, the member receives a coverage start date and information from Service Canada and Sun Life. Do not book treatment before coverage begins. CDCP does not reimburse members who pay the full bill themselves; the provider submits eligible claims directly.

[cards]
  - AFNI BELOW $70,000 — CDCP covers 100% of eligible services at CDCP established fees; additional provider charges can still remain.
  - AFNI $70,000 TO $79,999 — CDCP covers 60% of established fees and the member co-payment is 40%, plus possible additional charges.
  - AFNI $80,000 TO $89,999 — CDCP covers 40% of established fees and the member co-payment is 60%, plus possible additional charges.
  - AFNI $90,000 OR MORE — The income requirement is not met for that benefit period.

[spotlight] A provider’s usual fee may be higher than the CDCP established fee. Ask whether the office will charge the difference—often called balance billing—before treatment.

[spotlight] CDCP can coordinate with certain provincial, territorial and federal dental programs. Coordination rules vary, so show every coverage letter to the provider.

[myths]
  - “A 0% co-payment means the appointment must cost the member nothing.” — Zero per cent applies to eligible CDCP services at established fees. Uncovered services and provider fees above the CDCP amount can still create a bill.

[source] Canada: CDCP coverage
  url: https://www.canada.ca/en/services/benefits/dental/dental-care-plan/coverage.html

[spotlight] Preauthorization is a coverage review, not permission for treatment and not a promise that every future charge will be paid. The provider submits it with supporting documents.

[spotlight] Frequency limits matter. A service can appear in the benefits guide and still be unavailable because the allowed interval or quantity has already been used.

[myths]
  - “If a dentist recommends a service, CDCP must cover it.” — Clinical recommendation and benefit coverage answer different questions. The provider decides care with the patient; the plan applies coverage criteria.

[spotlight] Key takeaway: CDCP membership is not a blank cheque. Confirm the coverage date, service code, preauthorization, established fee, co-payment and provider difference before treatment begins.

## Renée and the Crown With Two Prices

[story] Meet Renée
  detail: DEDICATED STORY
  ¶ Renée Gagnon in Moncton had CDCP coverage and needed a crown. The clinic said the service was “covered,” a word Renée’s budget interpreted as “please leave your wallet in the car.”
  ¶ The conflict appeared in the estimate. The crown required preauthorization, Renée had a 40% co-payment and the clinic’s fee exceeded the CDCP established amount. Covered did not mean fully paid.
  ¶ The provider submitted the request, then gave Renée a written breakdown: plan payment, co-payment, fee difference and an alternative treatment discussion. Renée waited for the coverage decision before choosing with her dentist.
  ¶ The crown did not become inexpensive by vocabulary. It became understandable, which is the useful first step between a treatment plan and a payment plan.

[spotlight] Never rely on a verbal percentage alone. Ask for the procedure codes and member estimate so the provider and patient are discussing the same service, tooth and fee.

[heading] What resolved the conflict?

[paragraph] Covered is a coverage category, not a price. Confirm the codes, preauthorization and fee breakdown in writing before agreeing to treatment.

## Quick Check: Choose the Best Next Move

[accordions]
  - What four requirements form the CDCP eligibility screen? — No access to private dental insurance, required Canadian tax returns filed, adjusted family net income below $90,000 and Canadian residence.
  - Why can a member with a 0% co-payment still owe money? — Zero per cent applies to eligible CDCP services at established fees; uncovered services and provider fees above the CDCP amount can still create a bill.
  - Who submits an eligible CDCP claim? — The participating provider submits eligible claims directly; CDCP does not reimburse members who pay the full bill themselves.
  - What does preauthorization decide—and not decide? — It is a coverage review completed before specified treatment; it is not permission for treatment and not a promise that every future charge will be paid.
  - Why should all provincial or federal dental coverage be disclosed? — CDCP can coordinate with certain provincial, territorial and federal dental programs, and coordination rules vary, so every coverage letter should reach the provider.

## Glossary of Terms

[glossary]
  - CDCP — Canadian Dental Care Plan.
  - AFNI — Adjusted family net income.
  - Established fee — Amount CDCP uses to calculate payment.
  - Co-payment — Member percentage of the CDCP established fee.
  - Balance billing — Provider charge above the plan's established fee.
  - Preauthorization — Coverage review completed before specified treatment.
  - Frequency limit — Maximum number or interval for covered services.
