## Start here

[paragraph] A few years ago, first-time buyer support in Canada was short enough to summarize in one sentence: an RRSP withdrawal, a small tax credit, done. That sentence no longer describes anything.

[paragraph] These tools were built to stack, and the order you use them in changes what a house actually costs you.

[heading] What this course does

[paragraph] A new account arrived in 2023, the RRSP withdrawal limit nearly doubled in 2024, a federal sales tax rebate arrived in 2025, and Ontario added its own on a one-year window in 2026. This module is not a list to memorize. It is the order to work through before you sign anything.

## Before you buy: two accounts, working together

[paragraph] The First Home Savings Account, or FHSA, combines two things you already know from other registered accounts. Contributions are tax-deductible, like an RRSP. Qualifying withdrawals are tax-free, like a TFSA. You can put in up to eight thousand dollars a year, to a lifetime cap of forty thousand, and the account can stay open up to fifteen years.

[paragraph] The Home Buyers’ Plan lets you withdraw from an existing RRSP instead: up to sixty thousand dollars, tax-free at the time of withdrawal, but with an obligation attached. You repay it to your RRSP over fifteen years, and any year you miss the repayment gets added to your taxable income.

[paragraph] Here is the part that surprises people: you can use both, on the same home. A single buyer can draw on up to one hundred thousand dollars between the two. Two first-time buyers purchasing together can draw on up to two hundred thousand.

[cards]
  - Do you repay it — FHSA: No — Home Buyers’ Plan (RRSP): Yes, over 15 years, or it becomes taxable
  - Contribution needed first — FHSA: Yes, built by saving into it over time — Home Buyers’ Plan (RRSP): No, draws on RRSP room you may already have
  - Deadline pressure — FHSA: Must be closed by year-end after first withdrawal — Home Buyers’ Plan (RRSP): Repayments start the second year after withdrawal

[myths]
  - “The FHSA and the Home Buyers’ Plan are the same thing with different names.” — One is money you save specifically for this purpose and never repay. The other is a loan from your own retirement savings that you must repay on schedule. Using both together is smart. Confusing them is how people end up owing money back to an RRSP they thought was theirs free and clear.

[heading] CREDIT PULSE SPOTLIGHT: OPEN THE FHSA BEFORE YOU THINK YOU NEED IT

[spotlight] Contribution room does not appear until the account exists, and unused room only carries forward once the account is open. Someone who opens an FHSA the year they finally start seriously saving has already lost every year they could have been accumulating room quietly in the background, for free, with no obligation to contribute anything yet. The fix costs nothing. Open the account as soon as you are eligible, even with a small first deposit, even if a purchase is years away. You lose nothing by having it sit there. You lose real room by waiting.

## At closing: what comes off the price of a new build

[paragraph] If you are buying resale, skip ahead: none of this applies. This layer is specifically for new construction, and it is the part that changed the most recently.

[paragraph] The GST/HST New Housing Rebate has existed for years and reduces the sales tax on a new or substantially renovated home. A separate First-Time Home Buyers’ rebate took effect for agreements signed on or after March 20, 2025, eliminating the federal portion of the tax entirely on homes up to one million dollars, with the relief phasing out on homes up to one and a half million.

[paragraph] Ontario layered its own version on top in 2026: a temporary rebate of the full provincial portion of the HST, on the same terms, for agreements signed between April 1, 2026 and March 31, 2027 only. Combined, a first-time buyer of a qualifying new Ontario home in that window can see tax relief up to roughly one hundred thirty thousand dollars. Outside that window, or outside Ontario, the numbers are smaller. Check the federal rebate alone.

[paragraph] Two details decide whether any of this reaches you. First, ask your builder directly whether the rebate is credited at closing or whether you apply to the CRA yourself afterward; advertised new-build prices sometimes already assume the builder will claim it, which changes what you actually owe. Second, the rebate generally requires only one purchaser to be first-time. If you are buying with a partner who has owned before, get advice before assuming you qualify.

[myths]
  - “New builds are always more expensive than resale, so none of this matters to me.” — It changes the comparison, not eliminates it. A new home with a six-figure tax rebate attached can land closer to a comparable resale price than the sticker suggests. Whether that math works depends entirely on your city and your builder’s pricing, but it is worth running the numbers before ruling new construction out on price alone.

## Also at closing: land transfer tax rebates

[paragraph] Separate from the sales tax rebates above, and available on new builds and resale alike. Ontario, British Columbia and Prince Edward Island each offer a first-time buyer rebate on provincial land transfer tax, and Toronto layers a municipal rebate on top of the provincial one for its own Municipal Land Transfer Tax. Other provinces either have no land transfer tax or no first-time buyer exemption. Check your own before assuming it applies.

[paragraph] These rebates are usually applied by your lawyer directly at closing rather than claimed afterward, which is the one piece of good news in this section: if your lawyer knows to apply for it, you often never see the tax in the first place.

## Does this apply to me?

[chips]
  - I have not owned and lived in a home, as a principal residence, in the current year or the prior four, for myself and my spouse or partner.
  - I have an FHSA open, even if it is not yet close to full.
  - I know whether I am buying new construction or resale, because it changes which rebates exist.
  - If buying new, I have asked the builder directly how the rebate is applied.
  - I know whether my province has a land transfer tax rebate for first-time buyers.
  - I have not assumed my situation matches what a friend or relative describes. Several of these rules changed in the last two years.

[heading] CREDIT PULSE SPOTLIGHT: ONE PROGRAM YOU MAY HAVE HEARD OF THAT NO LONGER EXISTS

[spotlight] The federal First-Time Home Buyer Incentive, which had the government co-invest in your home in exchange for a share of future appreciation, ended new applications in March 2024. It still turns up in articles, videos and well-meaning advice from people who used it years ago. This is the recurring lesson of this entire course: a program having existed is not evidence that it exists now. Before you plan around anything in this module, or anything a relative mentions from their own purchase, check the date on the source. If an article does not carry one from the last year, treat every number in it as a starting point for verification rather than a fact.

## The order that actually works

[ordered]
  - Open an FHSA now, regardless of your timeline — Room only accumulates once the account exists.
  - Check your RRSP room and whether the Home Buyers’ Plan helps — Remember it is a loan to yourself with a real repayment schedule, not a grant.
  - Decide new construction or resale before you shop — It determines which entire layer of this module applies to you.
  - If buying new, ask the builder in writing how the rebate is handled — Confirm your agreement’s signing date against the current rebate windows.
  - Confirm your province’s land transfer tax rebate with your lawyer before closing — Some rebates must be claimed at the time of registration, not after.
  - Keep every confirmation in writing — Verbal assurances from a builder’s sales office are not what the CRA or your province will accept later.

## Aisha and Tom, a spreadsheet, and the year they nearly skipped

[story] Aisha and Tom’s spreadsheet
  detail: DEDICATED STORY
  ¶ Aisha and Tom had been renting in Ottawa for six years and told each other, every January, that this was the year they would start saving seriously. In practice, start saving meant a high-interest savings account that mostly funded vacations. Neither had opened an FHSA, because neither felt ready to use one.
  ¶ A colleague of Aisha’s mentioned, almost in passing, that the point of the account was to open it early and let the room build regardless of readiness. That one sentence cost them nothing to act on. They opened two accounts that week with four hundred dollars each and kept contributing modestly for the next two years, while their actual saving happened in the account that was doing something with it instead of just holding it.
  ¶ When they were finally ready to buy, in 2026, they were looking at a new-construction townhouse in Ontario. Their real estate lawyer was the one who flagged that their agreement, if signed before April 1, would miss Ontario’s temporary rebate window entirely, a difference of tens of thousands of dollars for the sake of a closing date. They pushed the signing back two weeks.
  ¶ Between the two FHSAs, a partial Home Buyers’ Plan withdrawal, the federal and provincial sales tax rebates, and the Ontario land transfer tax rebate, the combined support covered a share of the purchase that neither of them would have believed possible the year they were still telling each other next January.

[takeaway] Open an FHSA the moment you are eligible, whether or not you are close to buying. Room only builds once the account exists. Combine it with the Home Buyers’ Plan if it helps, but remember one is a loan to yourself. If you are buying new, ask in writing how the sales tax rebate is applied and check your signing date against current windows. And treat any figure from a source without a recent date as unverified.

## Glossary

[glossary]
  - FHSA — First Home Savings Account. Tax-deductible contributions, tax-free qualifying withdrawals, no repayment obligation.
  - HBP — Home Buyers’ Plan. An RRSP withdrawal for a first home, repaid over 15 years or taxed as income.
  - GST/HST New Housing Rebate — Federal sales tax relief on new or substantially renovated homes, with an enhanced first-time buyer version since March 2025.
  - Land transfer tax rebate — A provincial or municipal exemption on the tax paid when a property changes hands, for qualifying first-time buyers.
  - Particular time test — The CRA’s rule checking whether you or your spouse owned and occupied a home in the current year or the prior four.
  - First-Time Home Buyer Incentive — The closed federal shared-equity program. No longer accepting applications since March 2024.

[lists]
  - WHERE TO GET HELP — Canada Revenue Agency: 1-800-959-8281, for the FHSA, the Home Buyers’ Plan and the GST/HST rebates.; A real estate lawyer: for land transfer tax rebates, usually applied at closing rather than claimed afterward.; Your provincial finance ministry: to confirm whether a land transfer tax rebate exists where you are buying.

[paragraph] This module keeps dollar figures on purpose, because the recent changes are the point. Every one of them is time-limited or under active revision. Confirm current amounts and windows on canada.ca and your provincial government site before you rely on them.
