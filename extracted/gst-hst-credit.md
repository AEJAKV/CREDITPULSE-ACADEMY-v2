## Start here

[paragraph] The GST/HST Credit changed its name in July 2026. Your need to file taxes did not receive the rebrand memo.

[paragraph] The old GST/HST Credit is now the Canada Groceries and Essentials Benefit, or CGEB. It is still a tax-free payment for people and families with low or modest incomes, worked out from adjusted family net income. The July 2026 change added a 25% increase and kept the same four payments a year.

[spotlight] The last regular GST/HST Credit payment came in April 2026. Payments from July 2026 show up as the Canada Groceries and Essentials Benefit. Old notices still matter when you trace earlier periods.

## Track the Benefit Across the Name Change

[paragraph] Same benefit, new name tag. Read the timeline before you assume there's a second application.

[cards]
  - BEFORE JULY 2026 — The GST/HST Credit paid tax-free amounts four times a year. Older notices and account entries still use the old name.
  - JUNE 2026 — A one-time top-up went to eligible people. Don't mix it up with the regular quarterly payment.
  - FROM JULY 2026 — The CGEB replaced the old name, with a 25% increase meant to last five years, from 2026 to 2031.
  - EVERY JULY — A new July-to-June payment year starts, based on the tax return you filed the year before. July 2026 payments use your 2025 income (AFNI).

[spotlight] For July 2026 to June 2027, the published maximums are up to $679 for a single person, $890 for a married or common-law household and $234 for each eligible child under 19. Income and household facts can lower the amount.

[spotlight] Most people don't send in a separate CGEB application. Filing a tax return gives the CRA what it needs, even when your income is zero and the return feels like a very formal blank page.

[myths]
  - “Only people who paid a large amount of GST or HST can qualify.” — The benefit isn't based on saved receipts or the sales tax you paid. Your amount comes mainly from your tax and family information under the program rules.

## Owen and the Benefit With Two Name Tags

[story] Meet Owen
  detail: DEDICATED STORY
  ¶ Owen Park fixed bikes at a shop in Vancouver, and he rode one to work every day. His goal that July was simple. The chain on his own bike was nearly worn through, the repair cost $240, and his July GST/HST Credit was going to pay for it.
  ¶ Then he opened his CRA account. No GST/HST Credit. Instead, there was a payment called the Canada Groceries and Essentials Benefit. He figured the old credit was gone and the new one needed an application, because apparently one benefit wearing two name tags was not enough excitement for a Tuesday.
  ¶ He searched online. Three sites offered to "help him apply," and every one wanted a fee. The first wanted $79 up front. That night his chain slipped twice on the ride home, so the pressure felt real.
  ¶ He had his card out and the form half filled in. Then he stopped. At the shop, he always told customers to read the tag before they paid for a part. He closed the page and opened his CRA notice instead.
  ¶ The notice settled the big question. The CGEB was his old credit under a new name. He had filed his 2025 return, and the July payment used it. The June top-up was a separate one-time payment. There was no second application to buy.
  ¶ But the notice had one more surprise. It listed him as single. Owen had moved in with his partner, Sam, in May and never told the CRA. A change in marital status should be reported by the end of the following month, and it changes the household numbers the CRA uses.
  ¶ He updated his status in CRA My Account that same night and saved the notice. Then he wrote July, October, January and April on a sticky note and set a yearly reminder to file his return. His next deposit covered the chain, with money left over for a new set of brake pads. He kept the $79 in his pocket, which felt better than any chain ever has.

## File, Map and Update

[ordered]
  - File — You and your spouse or common-law partner should each file a tax return every year. The CRA uses your family income (AFNI), marital status and children to work out your household amount.
  - Map — The 2026-27 year pays out in July and October 2026, then January and April 2027. If your amount works out to less than $50 per quarter, the CRA may pay all of it in July.
  - Apply as a newcomer — If you're new to Canada and can't apply for the CCB, you may use form RC151. If you can apply for the CCB, use RC66. That form also sets up your CGEB and related benefits.
  - Update — Tell the CRA when your address, marital status, custody or other facts change. A recalculation can mean extra money, or an overpayment you owe back.
  - Verify — Trust your CRA notice and CRA account, not a payment screenshot from social media. Similar households can get different amounts because income and family facts differ.

[spotlight] Only one CGEB payment goes to a married or common-law couple. The CRA picks who gets it. One payment doesn't mean only one partner's income counted.

[spotlight] If a payment looks missing, check your filing status, CRA mail, address, marital information, debts or overpayments, and the expected date before you file a second application.

[myths]
  - “Moving in with a partner affects only next year’s benefit.” — Tell the CRA about a marital-status change by the end of the following month. The CRA may recalculate your current payments using your combined family information.

## Quick Check: Choose the Best Next Move

[accordions]
  - What changed in July 2026, and what stayed the same? — The GST/HST Credit was renamed the CGEB and went up 25%. The four payments a year and the need to file a tax return stayed the same.
  - Why is the June 2026 top-up separate from the July payment? — It was a one-time extra amount. It isn't part of the regular quarterly payments that began under the CGEB name in July.
  - Which tax return drives the July 2026-to-June 2027 payments? — Your 2025 tax return.
  - When would a newcomer use RC151 instead of RC66? — Use RC151 if you're becoming a Canadian resident and can't apply for the CCB. Use RC66 if you can apply for the CCB, since that form also sets up your related benefit information.
  - What should you check before assuming a payment is missing? — Your filing status, CRA mail, address, marital information, any debts or overpayments, and the expected payment date.

## Glossary of Terms

[glossary]
  - CGEB — Canada Groceries and Essentials Benefit. It replaced the GST/HST Credit.
  - Quarterly — Paid four times during the July-to-June benefit year.
  - AFNI — Adjusted family net income. The number the CRA uses to work out your amount.
  - Top-up — A one-time extra payment that is not one of the regular four.
  - RC151 — The CGEB application form for some people becoming Canadian residents.
  - RC66 — The CCB application form. It can also set up your related benefit information.
  - Overpayment — Benefit money paid above what you were due, which the CRA may take back.
