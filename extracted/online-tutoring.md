## Start here

[paragraph] Knowing algebra and teaching algebra are cousins, not twins. One solves for x; the other calmly explains why x keeps leaving the house without telling anyone.

[paragraph] Online tutoring is a service business built around a specific learner, subject and measurable outcome. A strong tutor does not promise guaranteed grades. They assess the starting point, teach a focused skill, check understanding and communicate progress. When working with minors, the tutor also needs parent or guardian consent, safe communication rules and privacy-conscious technology.

[spotlight] A narrow offer is easier to sell and deliver: for example, Grade 9 Ontario math problem solving or CELPIP speaking feedback—not “all subjects, all ages, all planets.”

[chips]
  - Choose a learner, subject and outcome.
  - Design a repeatable session arc.
  - Set pricing, policies and progress measures.
  - Protect student privacy and copyrighted material.
  - Complete a First Student Learning Studio Plan.

[myths]
  - “A tutor must know every answer immediately.” — A credible tutor can pause, verify and model how to find an answer. Confidently inventing a formula is performance art, not instruction.

## Sell Progress That Can Be Observed

[paragraph] A tutoring offer becomes sellable the moment progress can be shown, not just felt.

[cards]
  - LEARNER — Choose an age group, curriculum, exam, language level or professional skill you can support accurately.
  - STARTING POINT — Use a short diagnostic, sample work and learner conversation. Do not turn the first meeting into an ambush exam with dramatic music.
  - OUTCOME — Describe what the learner should be able to do: solve a type of problem, organize an essay, explain a concept or improve a practice score.
  - EVIDENCE — Compare attempts, error patterns, completed practice and learner explanations. Grades may matter, but they are affected by more than tutoring.

[spotlight] A useful 60-minute session can follow five moves: check-in, retrieve prior learning, model one skill, practise with feedback and assign a small next step.

[spotlight] For minors, keep parent or guardian consent, scheduling and payment in an adult-controlled channel. Agree on recording, messaging, missed sessions and who receives progress updates.

[myths]
  - “More worksheets automatically create more learning.” — Practice helps when it targets the learner's actual error and includes feedback. A 42-page packet can also become a very effective coaster.

[spotlight] Tutoring that follows a provincial curriculum, supports certain credit courses or meets specified prerequisite rules may be GST/HST-exempt. Other instruction may be taxable; the label “tutor” does not decide the answer.

[spotlight] The $30,000 small-supplier threshold applies to taxable supplies, not automatically to exempt tutoring revenue. Classify the service before using the threshold.

[myths]
  - “All tutoring is automatically GST/HST-exempt.” — The exemption depends on what is taught and how it connects to a designated curriculum, credit course or qualifying prerequisite. Verify the actual supply.

[spotlight] Key takeaway: A tutoring business earns trust by narrowing the offer, diagnosing before teaching, measuring one clear learning result, protecting young learners and operating with written pricing, privacy, copyright and cancellation rules.

## Eli Teaches Less and Learns More

[story] Meet Eli
  detail: DEDICATED STORY
  ¶ Eli Tremblay began tutoring high-school math online from Moncton. His first advertisement offered calculus, chemistry, essay writing and “general academic confidence.” It sounded impressive and slightly like four businesses sharing one trench coat.
  ¶ The conflict arrived during a Grade 9 session. Eli had prepared advanced equations, but his learner, Sophie, was losing marks because she skipped signs when moving terms. More content would not solve a smaller, repeatable error.
  ¶ Eli used three sample problems, asked Sophie to explain each step and built a one-page sign-check routine. He sent her parent a brief progress note, scheduled two focused sessions and rewrote his offer around Grade 9 and 10 math problem solving.
  ¶ Sophie became more accurate, and Eli gained a service he could explain, repeat and improve. His new slogan was not literally “fewer subjects, fewer trench coats,” but the principle remained sound.

[spotlight] The first session should produce a small win and a next-step diagnosis. A learner who understands the plan is more likely to practise between sessions.

[heading] What resolved the conflict?

[paragraph] A narrower offer diagnosed the real error instead of adding more content on top of it.

## Quick Check: Choose the Best Next Move

[accordions]
  - Why is a narrow tutoring offer easier to operate? — It is easier to sell and deliver because the tutor can accurately support one age group, curriculum, exam or skill instead of promising every subject to every learner.
  - What should happen before a tutor chooses the lesson? — A short diagnostic, sample work and learner conversation to establish the starting point.
  - Why should grades not be guaranteed? — Grades are evidence of progress but are affected by more than tutoring, so outcomes should describe an observable skill instead.
  - What privacy controls matter when tutoring a minor? — Parent or guardian consent, scheduling and payment kept in an adult-controlled channel, plus agreed rules on recording, messaging and progress updates.
  - Why must GST/HST treatment be checked rather than assumed? — The exemption depends on what is taught and how it connects to a designated curriculum, credit course or qualifying prerequisite—not the label “tutor.”

## Glossary of Terms

[glossary]
  - Diagnostic — Short assessment used to identify a learner's starting point.
  - Learning outcome — Specific ability the learner should demonstrate.
  - Session arc — Repeatable sequence used to structure a lesson.
  - Retrieval practice — Recalling prior learning without first reviewing the answer.
  - Progress note — Brief record of evidence, feedback and next step.
  - Exempt supply — Service on which GST/HST is generally not charged.
  - CASL — Canadian rules governing commercial electronic messages.
