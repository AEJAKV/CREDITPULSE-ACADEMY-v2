## Showroom brief

[paragraph] A car payment can look impressively small when the loan term has been stretched across several birthdays.

[paragraph] Compare offers by total cost, not showroom tempo. Separate price from financing, expose negative equity and add-ons, and read the disclosure. Then control inquiries and test whether the payment survives insurance, fuel, maintenance and an inconvenient Tuesday.

[spotlight] The payment is one line. Amount financed, APR, term and total payments reveal what the loan costs in full.

[lists]
  - Build an auto-loan readiness file before entering a dealership. — Calculate how trade-in debt, fees and add-ons change amount financed. — Compare shorter and longer terms using total payments and interest. — Identify negative equity and long-loan risk. — Leave with a total-cost comparison sheet and walk-away ceiling.

[myths]
  - “The lowest payment is the cheapest offer.” — A longer term can reduce payment while increasing total interest and time owing more than the vehicle is worth.

## Build the Price Before Discussing the Payment

[paragraph] If every number is folded into one weekly payment, the paperwork has learned camouflage.

[cards]
  - (1) — START WITH CASH PRICE — Negotiate and record vehicle price before financing. Confirm whether rebates depend on a particular loan.
  - (2) — SEPARATE THE TRADE-IN — Record trade value and exact old-loan payout. A higher payout creates negative equity that may enter the new loan.
  - (3) — LIST FINANCED EXTRAS — Write taxes, administration, registration, warranty, protection, insurance products and every optional add-on.
  - (4) — READ CREDIT TERMS — Compare amount financed, APR, frequency, payment count, term and total borrowing cost.
  - (5) — ADD OWNERSHIP COSTS — Get an insurance quote and estimate fuel or charging, tires, parking, maintenance and repairs.

[spotlight] Negative equity does not vanish when rolled in. It becomes old-car debt travelling inside the new-car payment.

[paragraph] Gather identification, income and residence proof, current payout, insurance quote and recent reports. Get financial-institution and dealer quotes where practical; compare the same vehicle price, down payment and term.

[spotlight] FCAC says car-loan or mortgage quotes from different lenders within a two-week period are treated by the bureaus as one inquiry.

[myths]
  - “A dealership must show me its lowest available rate.” — The presented offer may not be the best available. Compare quotes and ask which lender, rate, fees, compensation and conditions apply.

[source] FCAC: Car financing options
  url: https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/loans/financing-car/financing-options.html

## Lucas and the $119 Weekly Mirage

[story] Meet Lucas (DEDICATED STORY)

Lucas Martin, a refrigeration technician in Regina, needed a reliable vehicle for early calls. His hatchback was approaching retirement with the dignity of a shopping cart on one stubborn wheel.

A dealer offered a compact SUV for $119 weekly. The payment looked comfortable and the heated seats made an unusually powerful Saskatchewan argument. Then Lucas requested the worksheet: 84 months, $4,000 trade-in negative equity, paint protection, replacement insurance and an unpriced warranty.

Lucas took the quote home and rebuilt it: cash price, trade, payout, shortfall, taxes, fees, add-ons, financed amount, APR and total payments. A bank quote and insurance estimate showed the weekly payment fit, but total ownership did not. The long loan could keep the former hatchback travelling inside the SUV for years.

Lucas returned with price and financing ceilings, removed extras and declined to roll the shortfall into a costlier vehicle. He kept the hatchback three months while paying it down, then bought a lower-priced used crossover over five years.

The payment was higher than the advertised weekly number, but total borrowing cost and negative-equity risk were lower. No one applauded when he signed; he did know the price, rate, term, ownership cost and exit risk.


[spotlight] Lucas controlled the five places a small payment hides a large obligation: price, trade, add-ons, financed amount and term.

[heading] What resolved the conflict?

[paragraph] A printed quote, outside financing and a walk-away ceiling turned pressure into a comparison he could finish at home.

[transition] The 84-month loan and the 60-month loan differ by one thing: about $3,010 in interest.

## Total-Cost Lab: Expose the 84-Month Mirage

[paragraph] The same $35,000 amount financed and 7.49% APR. Figures are rounded; the actual disclosure controls.

[cards]
  - 84 months — MONTHLY $536.67 — TOTAL PAYMENTS $45,080.02 — INTEREST $10,080.02
  - 60 months — MONTHLY $701.16 — TOTAL PAYMENTS $42,069.71 — INTEREST $7,069.71
  - Difference — MONTHLY $164.49 lower — TOTAL PAYMENTS $3,010.31 higher — INTEREST $3,010.31 more

[spotlight] The longer loan buys payment relief, not a discount. If the short payment does not fit, the safer lever may be a lower vehicle price.

[heading] Before signing

[ordered]
  - Read the disclosure statement showing total borrowing cost and keep a copy.
  - Confirm contract price, trade, payout, add-ons, APR, term and frequency.
  - Ask whether optional products can be removed and how cancellation or refunds affect the loan.
  - Do not assume next-day cooling off; rules vary by province or territory.

[myths]
  - “I can always cancel the vehicle contract tomorrow.” — There is no universal Canadian next-day cancellation rule. Check the contract and provincial or territorial law before signing.

## Quick Check: Choose the Best Next Move

[accordions]
  - How much more interest does the 84-month loan cost? — About $3,010.31 more than the 60-month loan.
  - What happens when $4,000 negative equity is rolled forward? — It becomes additional principal in the new loan, increasing amount financed and usually interest and underwater risk.
  - Which document should show total borrowing cost? — The disclosure statement provided before the agreement is finalized.
  - How can you limit scoring effect while comparing quotes? — Shop lender quotes within a focused two-week period; FCAC says car-loan quotes in that period are treated as one inquiry by the bureaus.
  - What should you verify before assuming cancellation? — The signed contract and current provincial or territorial dealer and consumer law; there is no universal next-day Canadian cancellation right.

## Glossary of Terms

[glossary]
  - Amount financed — The principal borrowed after the down payment, trade, fees, taxes, add-ons and rolled-in debt are applied.
  - APR — Annual percentage rate: the annualized cost of credit used to compare loan offers.
  - Negative equity — The amount by which a vehicle-loan balance exceeds the vehicle’s value.
  - Loan term — The period over which scheduled payments are made.
  - Lien — A registered lender claim against the vehicle until the secured debt is repaid.
  - Disclosure statement — The pre-contract document explaining the cost of borrowing and key credit terms.
