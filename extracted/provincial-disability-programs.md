## Start here

[paragraph] A disability can cross a provincial border. Its benefit file, less adventurous, usually has to stop and ask for directions.

[paragraph] Every province and territory designs its own disability-income and health-support programs. Ontario uses ODSP; Alberta operates AISH and ADAP; British Columbia requires a Persons with Disabilities designation; Quebec uses Social Solidarity and, for some long-term recipients, the Basic Income Program. These names describe distinct legal and financial tests—not regional nicknames for one national benefit.

[spotlight] Federal DTC or CPP disability approval may help with some provincial routes, but it does not automatically establish eligibility everywhere. Each program decides which outside approvals it recognizes.

[chips]
  - Identify the correct local disability program.
  - Separate disability designation from financial eligibility.
  - Organize functional, medical and financial evidence.
  - Compare earnings, health and review rules.
  - Complete a Provincial Disability Support Comparison File.

[myths]
  - “A disability-benefit approval transfers automatically when a person moves provinces.” — The medical condition moves; the provincial entitlement generally does not. A new jurisdiction may require a new application, designation and financial review.

[source] Ontario: ODSP
  url: https://www.ontario.ca/page/ontario-disability-support-program

## Disability Status Is Only One Gate

[paragraph] A medical form opens one gate. These four decide whether it leads anywhere.

[cards]
  - RESIDENCE AND AGE — Confirm where the applicant lives, minimum age, legal-status rules and transition points for youth or seniors.
  - DISABILITY DEFINITION — Read the local duration, severity, functional impact and employability test. Do not paste CPP disability wording into an ODSP, AISH, ADAP, PWD or Social Solidarity application.
  - FINANCIAL ELIGIBILITY — Income, partner income, assets, trusts, RDSPs, gifts and settlements may be treated differently. Medical approval alone may not produce a payment.
  - ONGOING ADMINISTRATION — Work income, monthly or annual reporting, medical reviews, travel, household changes and health-benefit continuation can affect support after approval.

[spotlight] Ontario’s ODSP generally applies both a disability definition and financial eligibility. B.C. similarly separates PWD designation from the financial test for disability assistance.

[spotlight] Alberta launched ADAP in July 2026 alongside AISH, using one combined Disability Income Assistance application. The two programs have different work-capacity focus and earnings treatment.

[myths]
  - “Once the medical form is accepted, income and assets no longer matter.” — Most provincial disability-income programs remain financially tested. A designation can survive a temporary loss of payment in some jurisdictions, but the person must verify the local rule.

[source] Alberta: Disability Income Assistance
  url: https://www.alberta.ca/disability-income-assistance

## Use the Province's Definition as the Filing Spine

[paragraph] Begin with the current application guide. Build evidence under its actual headings: diagnosis and history, expected duration, treatment, daily function, assistance, work capacity and prognosis. The applicant explains lived impact; the authorized health professional supplies clinical evidence and judgment.

[spotlight] B.C. permits some prescribed classes to obtain PWD designation through a streamlined route based on another recognized approval. This is a B.C. rule—not permission to assume reciprocity across Canada.

[paragraph] Next build the financial file: bank statements, housing costs, income, assets, partner information, trusts, RDSPs, settlements and benefits. Label every figure with the policy date and exemption source. Ask how employment income affects cash support, health coverage and reporting separately.

[spotlight] Examples of work treatment differ: Ontario exempts the first $1,000 of monthly earnings for an ODSP recipient with a disability, while B.C. uses an annual earnings exemption—$16,200 for a single PWD recipient in 2026.

[myths]
  - “Working always causes immediate loss of disability support and health benefits.” — Programs may use earnings exemptions, partial reductions or extended health coverage. Report work accurately and request a written calculation before turning down employment based on folklore.

[spotlight] Provincial disability support requires a local comparison: confirm the disability definition, financial test, work exemptions, health coverage and review duties before applying or changing income.

## Marcus and the Approval That Packed Light

[story] Meet Marcus
  detail: DEDICATED STORY
  ¶ Marcus Bell moved from Toronto to Calgary with an ODSP approval letter and assumed Alberta would simply change the logo. The letter crossed the country beautifully. The entitlement had neglected to book a seat.
  ¶ The conflict was a different system. Alberta’s combined application assessed AISH and ADAP using Alberta definitions, finances and work-capacity rules. Marcus also needed to understand whether health coverage would continue during the transition.
  ¶ He obtained the current Alberta application, reused relevant medical records without assuming they answered every local question and created a fresh functional chronology. He reported the move to Ontario and asked both programs for written transition instructions.
  ¶ The process was not automatic, but it became manageable. Marcus replaced ‘transfer my benefit’ with four precise questions: which program, which definition, which financial test and what support covers the gap?

[spotlight] Before an interprovincial move, contact both jurisdictions about end dates, new applications, medication coverage, mobility equipment and emergency assistance. Do not rely on the payment calendar to coordinate itself.

[heading] What resolved the conflict?

[paragraph] Disability support is defined provincially. Confirm the local definition, the financial test and the transition plan before assuming an approval travels with you.

## Quick Check: Choose the Best Next Move

[accordions]
  - Why can federal disability approval fail to settle provincial eligibility? — Each province decides which outside approvals it recognizes; federal DTC or CPP disability approval may help but does not automatically establish eligibility everywhere.
  - What four gates belong in the comparison? — Residence and age, disability definition, financial eligibility and ongoing administration.
  - Why should medical and financial files be built separately? — The applicant explains lived impact and the authorized health professional supplies clinical evidence, while the financial file uses different documents and exemption rules; medical approval alone may not produce a payment.
  - How can earnings rules differ between Ontario and B.C.? — Ontario exempts the first $1,000 of monthly earnings for an ODSP recipient with a disability, while B.C. uses an annual earnings exemption of $16,200 for a single PWD recipient in 2026.
  - What should a learner verify before moving provinces? — Whether the new jurisdiction requires a new application, designation and financial review, and how end dates, medication coverage and emergency assistance are handled during the transition.

## Glossary of Terms

[glossary]
  - Disability designation — Formal provincial recognition under a local definition.
  - Financial eligibility — Income and asset test for benefit payment.
  - ODSP — Ontario Disability Support Program.
  - AISH / ADAP — Alberta disability-income programs with different work-capacity focus.
  - PWD designation — B.C. Persons with Disabilities status.
  - Earnings exemption — Work income excluded before benefit reduction under local rules.
  - Medical review — Reassessment of continuing disability-program eligibility.
