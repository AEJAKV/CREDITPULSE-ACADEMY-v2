## Start here

[paragraph] Canada has thirteen provincial and territorial benefit systems, because apparently one application vocabulary would have been dangerously convenient.

[paragraph] Provincial and territorial income-support programs provide last-resort help with basic needs such as food, clothing and shelter. Program names, rates, asset limits, work expectations, emergency help and appeal routes differ by jurisdiction. This lesson teaches a repeatable search and application method; it does not create one fictional national rule.

[spotlight] Start with the official website of the province or territory where the applicant lives. Federal Benefits Finder results can identify possibilities, but the local program makes the eligibility decision.

[chips]
  - Locate the correct provincial or territorial program.
  - Separate financial, household and participation rules.
  - Prepare an urgent-needs and standard application route.
  - Maintain an active file and challenge a decision.
  - Complete a Local Income Support Route Card.

[myths]
  - “Social assistance works the same everywhere in Canada.” — The purpose is similar, but names, rates, exemptions and responsibilities vary. Moving across a provincial border can change the rulebook without changing the grocery bill.

## Use Five Filters Before Reading a Rate

[paragraph] Run every situation through these five filters before comparing a rate table—the filters decide which door is correct, the rate table only decides how much is behind it.

[cards]
  - JURISDICTION — Confirm the province or territory of residence and whether on-reserve administration or another authority applies.
  - URGENCY — Separate immediate food, shelter, medication, utility or safety needs from the standard application. Ask about emergency assistance rather than assuming the regular queue is the only door.
  - HOUSEHOLD — Record adults, spouses or partners, dependants, custody, housing and immigration or sponsorship facts requested by the local rules.
  - RESOURCES — List income, cash, accounts, investments, vehicles, property and debts. Then classify them using the jurisdiction's exempt and non-exempt rules.
  - WORK CAPACITY AND PARTICIPATION — Some programs require employment planning, job search or referrals; temporary health, caregiving or safety circumstances may change those responsibilities.

[spotlight] Ontario Works, Alberta Income Support, B.C. Income Assistance and Quebec Social Assistance are different programs. Their official pages are examples—not interchangeable instruction manuals.

[spotlight] A rate page shows a maximum category, not necessarily the final payment. Shelter cost, household size, income, assets, deductions, sanctions and supplements can change the result.

[myths]
  - “Having a car, RRSP or small savings balance always causes denial.” — Each program defines assets and exemptions differently. Declare the resource, find the current rule and request a written calculation; do not hide it or disqualify yourself by guesswork.

[source] Alberta: Income Support
  url: https://www.alberta.ca/income-support

## An Approval Is the Start of Reporting

[paragraph] A complete application commonly needs identity and status documents, proof of residence, household information, rent or shelter costs, bank statements, income and asset records, and information about work or immediate needs. Submit only through the program’s official channel and keep copies or confirmation numbers.

[spotlight] Alberta lists 24-hour emergency assistance for urgent basic needs, while its standard Income Support application may take longer. Other jurisdictions have their own emergency routes—ask directly and document the answer.

[paragraph] After approval, report changes by the local deadline. A failure to report can produce suspension or an overpayment even when the original application was accurate.

[spotlight] Keep every decision notice. It should identify the benefit period, facts used, deductions or overpayment and review or appeal deadline. A bank deposit cannot explain why it changed; it is a number, not a caseworker in disguise.

[myths]
  - “If a caseworker gives an answer by phone, there is no need to keep records.” — Record the date, office, question, answer and next step, then request written confirmation for material decisions. Memory is a poor appeal exhibit.

[spotlight] Provincial income support is local, last-resort and actively administered. Verify the current jurisdictional rules, separate emergency needs from the standard claim and maintain a dated evidence trail after approval.

## Kira and the Application From the Wrong Province

[story] Meet Kira
  detail: DEDICATED STORY
  ¶ Kira moved from Regina to Vancouver after a job ended. A friend sent her a Saskatchewan assistance checklist, and Kira began gathering documents for a program that no longer lived where she did. The form was thorough, official-looking and geographically ambitious.
  ¶ Her conflict was immediate: rent was due before a standard application would be assessed. Kira needed B.C.’s current route, not yesterday’s province and not a federal benefit page pretending to be a local decision.
  ¶ She used the B.C. government site, separated her urgent shelter need from the full income-assistance application and documented both contacts. She listed her small savings and vehicle, then checked their treatment under B.C.’s rules instead of assuming denial.
  ¶ Kira did not receive a universal Canadian answer. She received something more useful: the correct office, current checklist, emergency question and a confirmation trail tied to the province where she actually lived.

[spotlight] A move can end one jurisdiction’s assistance and require a fresh application elsewhere. Report the move to the former program and contact the new jurisdiction promptly to reduce gaps and overpayments.

## Quick Check: Choose the Best Next Move

[accordions]
  - Why is there no single Canadian income-support rate? — Each province and territory designs and administers its own program; only the general last-resort purpose is shared across jurisdictions.
  - Which five filters help locate the correct route? — Jurisdiction, urgency, household, resources and work capacity and participation.
  - Why should resources be declared before deciding whether they are exempt? — Exemption rules differ by jurisdiction, so declaring the resource and requesting a written calculation avoids wrongly assuming denial or hiding an asset by guesswork.
  - What changes commonly require reporting? — Work, earnings, gifts, benefits, household members, address, rent, bank balances, travel, school or medical circumstances.
  - What should be saved if a decision appears wrong? — The decision notice showing the benefit period, facts used, deductions or overpayment and the review or appeal deadline, plus a dated record of related conversations.

## Glossary of Terms

[glossary]
  - Income support — Provincial or territorial last-resort basic-needs assistance.
  - Jurisdiction — Government authority administering the program.
  - Liquid asset — Readily available money or property under local rules.
  - Exempt asset — Resource excluded from a particular eligibility calculation.
  - Benefit unit — People grouped together for the household calculation.
  - Overpayment — Assistance paid above revised entitlement.
  - Appeal — Formal process challenging an administrative decision.
