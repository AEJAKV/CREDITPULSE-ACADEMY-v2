## Workshop brief

[paragraph] A bankruptcy discharge certificate is important paperwork, but it does not arrive with a tiny marching band or an instant 800 credit score.

[paragraph] It does something more practical: it confirms a legal stage has ended, subject to debts that the law does not release.

[paragraph] This workshop begins after discharge. You will confirm your status, protect the documents that prove it, audit both credit reports, correct inaccuracies and build a modest credit system that can survive real life. No product can promise a score increase or an approval date. Your advantage is a clean sequence and records that support it.

[spotlight] Discharge, credit-report removal and credit recovery run on different clocks. Do not use one date as though it controls all three.

  - Your workshop outputs ["A discharge-status and surviving-debt checkpoint.","A two-bureau post-bankruptcy credit audit.","A correction package for inaccurate accounts or dates.","A safe-credit readiness test.","A 12-month re-entry plan with review dates rather than score promises."]

[myths]
  - “Discharge means every debt is gone and every creditor must stop reporting it immediately.” — A discharge releases many pre-bankruptcy debts, but the Bankruptcy and Insolvency Act excludes certain obligations. Credit-report retention is a separate issue.

## Noah’s First Credit Offer Was Not a Welcome Basket

[story] Meet Noah (DEDICATED STORY)
  - Noah Sinclair, a warehouse dispatcher in Winnipeg, completed a first bankruptcy after a job loss and a long stretch of reduced hours. When his discharge arrived, he expected the next credit decision to feel normal again. Instead, his mailbox produced an offer for a high-fee loan with a rate that seemed to have trained for the decathlon.
  - Noah wanted to rebuild quickly because he hoped to replace an aging vehicle the following year. The expensive offer felt like a shortcut. His Licensed Insolvency Trustee had warned him that discharge did not guarantee new credit, so Noah paused and built a re-entry folder instead. It contained his discharge certificate, statement of affairs, trustee correspondence and a list of debts included in the bankruptcy.
  - He then reviewed both Canadian credit reports. One bureau showed the bankruptcy dates he expected. The other still showed a pre-bankruptcy credit-card balance as currently past due. Noah did not assume the bureau would connect the dots by telepathy. He sent a dispute with the relevant report page and copies of his discharge and account documents. The entry was investigated and updated.
  - Before applying for anything, Noah built a $500 starter buffer and ran a three-month test: all ordinary bills on time, no overdraft and no reliance on credit for groceries. He then compared secured-card fees, reporting and deposit terms. He chose one manageable card, placed one small recurring bill on it and paid the statement balance in full each month. The security deposit did not pay the monthly bill; Noah did.
  - Nine months later, Noah still did not have a guaranteed vehicle approval, and he had stopped treating approval as the only measurement. His reports were accurate, his cash flow had room for a future payment, his card balance stayed low and his application plan compared total borrowing cost rather than the size of the weekly payment. He had not erased the bankruptcy. He had built evidence after it. Still no marching band, but nobody was waiting on one.

[spotlight] The first post-bankruptcy win may be an accurate report, a cash buffer or six clean due dates. A score is only one measurement of re-entry readiness.

[heading] Why the story concludes well

[paragraph] Noah resisted the product-first approach. He proved discharge, corrected the file, tested his budget and opened only one account he could manage. The conflict ended with a system, not a magical number.

## Gate 1: Confirm That the Legal Stage Is Actually Complete

[paragraph] This course assumes you have received a discharge. Filing for bankruptcy and being discharged are not the same event. If you are uncertain, contact the Licensed Insolvency Trustee who administered the estate before applying for new credit or telling a creditor that every obligation was released.

[heading] Debts that may survive discharge

[paragraph] OSB guidance lists obligations that are not released in the usual way, including support payments, court fines or penalties, debts arising from fraud and some student loans when fewer than seven years have passed since the person ceased being a student. Other facts can matter. Confirm your own list with the LIT or qualified legal advice rather than relying on a general course summary.

[spotlight] A discharge certificate proves a legal event. Keep it with the trustee’s contact details and the list of debts included in the estate.

[myths]
  - “If an account was listed in the bankruptcy, I can discard every related document.” — Those records may be needed to correct a report, answer a future lender’s document request or prove how an account was treated.

[heading] IF YOU ARE NOT DISCHARGED

[paragraph] Different rules apply.

[paragraph] OSB states that an undischarged bankrupt may not borrow more than $1,000 without informing the lender of the bankruptcy. Contact the LIT before borrowing.

[source] OSB: Considering bankruptcy
  url: https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/office-superintendent-bankruptcy/en/you-owe-money/considering-bankruptcy

## Gate 2: Audit Both Reports Line by Line

[paragraph] Equifax and TransUnion maintain separate files.

[paragraph] Review both; a correction at one bureau does not prove the other file changed.

[cards]
  - 01 | Identity section — Check names, addresses and other identifying details. Flag information that could mix your file with someone else’s.
  - 02 | Bankruptcy entry — Compare filing and discharge details with the trustee documents.
  - 03 | Included accounts — Check creditor name, balance, status and dates. Confirm whether new delinquency is being reported accurately after the insolvency event.
  - 04 | Surviving obligations — Separate debts that were not released from debts included in the discharge. Ask the LIT if the treatment is unclear.
  - 05 | Inquiries and unfamiliar accounts — Investigate applications you did not make and follow the fraud process where appropriate.
  - 06 | Dispute package — Use the bureau’s official procedure. Attach copies of the relevant page, discharge proof and supporting account documents; keep originals.

[spotlight] For a first bankruptcy, FCAC says Equifax and TransUnion usually remove the bankruptcy six years after discharge; TransUnion uses seven years in Newfoundland and Labrador, Ontario, Prince Edward Island and Quebec. Multiple bankruptcies may remain for 14 years. Recheck current bureau and provincial rules.

[myths]
  - “Once the bankruptcy entry is correct, every old account line must disappear.” — The report may continue to show historical accounts and statuses for the applicable period. The goal is accurate treatment, not the removal of accurate information.

[source] FCAC: How long information stays on your credit report
  url: https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/credit-reports-score/information-credit-report.html

[source] FCAC: Checking your credit report for errors and fraud
  url: https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/credit-reports-score/check-errors.html

## Gate 3: Pass the Safe-Credit Readiness Test

[paragraph] The safest time to add credit is when the account has a job, the cost is understood and the budget can pay it without using the account for essentials.

[heading] Five readiness questions

[ordered]
  - Are housing, food, utilities, transport, support obligations and required debt payments currently protected? — 
  - Is there a starter emergency buffer so one small surprise does not immediately return to credit? — 
  - Can the proposed account be paid on time from ordinary income, including its annual fee and security deposit? — 
  - Does the lender report the account to a Canadian credit bureau, and are the fees, rate and cancellation terms clear? — 
  - Can you wait if the product is expensive, confusing or being sold with a guaranteed-score claim? — 

[heading] If a secured card is selected

[paragraph] A secured card normally requires a deposit as security. The deposit is not a prepaid balance and does not replace the monthly payment. Use a small recurring charge, keep the reported balance manageable and pay on time. You do not need to carry interest-bearing debt to build payment history.

[spotlight] The security deposit protects the issuer. Your on-time payments build the new record.

[myths]
  - “Carrying a balance proves I can manage debt.” — Paying interest is not required to create payment history. Responsible use, on-time payments and manageable balances matter.

[heading] A practical 12-month sequence

[cards]
  - MONTHS 1–3 — Confirm discharge, audit both reports, correct errors and stabilize bills.
  - MONTHS 4–6 — Build the cash buffer and, if ready, manage one low-cost reporting account.
  - MONTHS 7–9 — Continue clean payments, review balances and avoid unnecessary applications.
  - MONTHS 10–12 — Review both reports again and compare any major-loan need using total cost, not approval alone.

## Quick Check: Choose the Best Next Move

[accordions]
  - You have filed but cannot find proof of discharge. Who should you contact first? => The Licensed Insolvency Trustee who administered the estate.
  - One bureau shows a pre-bankruptcy account as currently past due. What is the next step? => Gather the relevant report page, discharge proof and account records, then use that bureau’s official dispute process.
  - A secured card requires a deposit. Does the deposit make the monthly payment? => No. The deposit secures the issuer; the cardholder must still pay the card bill.
  - A lender guarantees a 100-point increase for a large fee. What should you do? => Walk away. No lender or product can guarantee a specific score increase.
  - What matters more than opening several accounts quickly? => Accurate reports, protected essentials, on-time payments, manageable balances and one account you can reliably control.

## Glossary

  - Bankruptcy — A legal insolvency process administered under Canada’s Bankruptcy and Insolvency Act.
  - Licensed Insolvency Trustee (LIT) — A federally regulated professional authorized to administer bankruptcies and consumer proposals.
  - Discharge — The legal release from the obligation to repay many debts included in the bankruptcy, subject to exceptions.
  - Absolute discharge — A discharge that takes effect without continuing conditions.
  - Surplus income — Income above the applicable OSB standard that can affect required payments and bankruptcy duration.
  - Secured credit card — A credit card backed by a security deposit; the card balance still requires payment.
  - Re-entry plan — A staged system for document control, report accuracy, cash-flow stability and careful credit use after discharge.
