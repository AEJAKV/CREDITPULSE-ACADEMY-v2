## Start here

[paragraph] Rent assistance has a small naming problem: the same idea may arrive as a benefit, subsidy, supplement, allowance or wait-list letter wearing sensible shoes.

[paragraph] Rent help in Canada is delivered through provincial, territorial, municipal, Indigenous and housing-provider programs. It may be portable cash assistance, rent-geared-to-income housing, a rent supplement attached to a unit or short-term eviction-prevention help. Availability, priorities and wait times differ locally; the right search begins with the household and housing situation.

[spotlight] The Canada Housing Benefit is delivered through thirteen tailored provincial and territorial initiatives. It is not one national application portal with one national rate.

[chips]
  - Calculate a housing-cost pressure snapshot.
  - Distinguish portable benefits from subsidized units.
  - Match age, family and priority categories to the right door.
  - Respond before rent arrears become an eviction crisis.
  - Complete a Rent Assistance Search and Stability File.

[myths]
  - “Anyone paying more than 30% of income on rent automatically receives a subsidy.” — Thirty per cent is a common affordability measure, not an approval code. Income limits, household priority, housing type, funding and wait-list rules still decide access.

## Four Doors Can Look Like Rent Help

[paragraph] Read the doors before assuming they all lead to the same room.

[cards]
  - PORTABLE RENT BENEFIT — A payment follows the eligible household in qualifying market housing, subject to local rules and renewal.
  - RENT-GEARED-TO-INCOME HOUSING — Rent is linked to household income in an approved unit. Access often involves a centralized or local wait list.
  - UNIT-BASED SUPPLEMENT — Funding is attached to a specific landlord, co-operative or housing provider. Moving may end the assistance.
  - EMERGENCY HOUSING HELP — Short-term funds, social assistance, rent banks or prevention services may address arrears, deposits or urgent shelter risk.

[spotlight] B.C. separates Rental Assistance Program support for eligible working families with children from SAFER support for eligible renters aged 60 or older. Household category changes the door.

[spotlight] Alberta offers monthly rent benefits and community-based affordable housing. Local housing providers may administer applications, so the provincial page is often the map—not the final counter.

[myths]
  - “Joining one housing wait list places me on every relevant list.” — Municipalities, housing providers and specialized programs may maintain separate applications. Ask which lists, buildings and portable benefits require distinct action.

[source] Alberta: Rent assistance
  url: https://www.alberta.ca/rent-assistance

## Measure the Gap Before Choosing the Door

[paragraph] Record gross and net household income, monthly rent, included utilities, unit size, accessibility needs, household members and any arrears. A simple pressure measure is rent divided by gross income, but use the program’s official income definition for eligibility.

[spotlight] A rent receipt proves payment; a lease proves the tenancy terms. Many applications need both, plus tax information, income evidence, status documents and household details.

[paragraph] If rent is late or an eviction notice has arrived, contact the landlord or housing provider and the local tenant, legal or housing-stability service immediately. Ask for a written repayment proposal and apply for emergency help. Do not confuse a subsidy wait list with an urgent eviction response.

[spotlight] Portable benefits can require annual income review and reporting of moves or household changes. Unit-based assistance may end when the tenancy ends. Read the renewal letter before giving notice to move.

[myths]
  - “A rent subsidy solves every housing-cost problem.” — Utilities, parking, insurance, moving costs and rent above program limits may remain. Build a full housing budget; the lease has supporting actors.

[heading] Key takeaway

[paragraph] Rent assistance is a local matching exercise. Measure the housing gap, identify whether support follows the household or the unit, join every relevant route and treat arrears as an urgent stability problem.

## Alicia and the Wait List That Wasn't a Rescue Boat

[story] Meet Alicia
  detail: DEDICATED STORY
  ¶ Alicia Morgan in Toronto applied for community housing and assumed the wait-list confirmation would protect her current apartment. When her hours fell, she missed part of the rent. The confirmation letter remained calm, laminated and entirely unable to negotiate with her landlord.
  ¶ Her conflict was timing. Long-term subsidized housing and immediate arrears prevention were different routes. Alicia also qualified to ask her local housing office about a portable benefit, but availability and priority rules required direct confirmation.
  ¶ She contacted a tenant-support clinic, proposed a written repayment schedule and gathered her lease, rent ledger, tax notice and reduced-hours letter. She kept the long-term application active while pursuing the urgent local options.
  ¶ Alicia did not turn a wait list into cash. She built parallel plans: protect today’s tenancy, pursue portable help and keep the long-term housing application current.

[spotlight] Update housing applications when phone, address, household, income or accessibility needs change. A closed file can spend years waiting perfectly in the wrong place.

## Quick Check: Choose the Best Next Move

[accordions]
  - Why is the Canada Housing Benefit not one national application? — It is delivered through thirteen tailored provincial and territorial initiatives, each with its own rules, portal and rate rather than one federal program.
  - What four rent-support doors should be compared? — Portable rent benefits, rent-geared-to-income housing, unit-based supplements and emergency housing help.
  - Which records establish income, tenancy and payment? — Tax information and income evidence establish income, a lease establishes tenancy terms, and a rent receipt or rent ledger establishes payment.
  - Why should an eviction risk not wait for a subsidy list? — A wait list addresses long-term housing over time, while an eviction notice is an immediate deadline; arrears and eviction risk need direct contact with the landlord and an emergency-help route right away.
  - What must be checked before moving from a subsidized unit? — Whether the assistance is unit-based, which would end at that address, or portable, and what the renewal letter requires before giving notice.

## Glossary of Terms

[glossary]
  - Portable benefit — Rent assistance that may move with an eligible household.
  - RGI — Rent-geared-to-income housing.
  - Unit-based supplement — Assistance attached to a specific rental unit.
  - Housing-cost ratio — Rent or shelter cost compared with household income.
  - Rent ledger — Landlord's record of charges and payments.
  - Arrears — Past-due rent.
  - Wait list — Queue for housing or assistance, subject to local priority rules.
