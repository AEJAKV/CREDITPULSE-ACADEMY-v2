## Start here

[paragraph] Snowball and avalanche are repayment orders, not different loans.

[paragraph] Both begin by keeping required payments current on every debt. The difference is where the planned extra money goes: snowball targets the smallest balance, while avalanche targets the highest interest rate.

[paragraph] In this Repayment Tournament, you will run the same debts through both strategies. Compare interest, timing and visible wins, then pick the route least likely to fail in your household. A mathematically elegant plan that gets abandoned is mostly elegant stationery.

[source] FCAC: Paying back your debt
  url: https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/debt/paying-debt.html

[spotlight] Both strategies keep required payments going on every debt and put the extra on one target. The tournament changes the target order, not the payment floor.

[heading] Meet the Two Contenders

[cards]
  - LAYER 1 — SNOWBALL — SMALLEST BALANCE FIRST — List debts from the smallest balance to the largest. Pay the required amount on every account and send the extra to the smallest. When it is paid off, roll its old payment into the next target. The early win can boost your motivation.
  - LAYER 2 — AVALANCHE — HIGHEST RATE FIRST — List debts from the highest interest rate to the lowest. Pay the required amount on every account and send the extra to the highest rate. Roll the paid-off payment forward. This method usually cuts interest and can shorten repayment.
  - LAYER 3 — ENTRY RULE — Deal with urgent past-due, essential, secured, collection or legal risks before you run the tournament. Course 2.8’s triage order still applies.

[myths]
  - “Avalanche is the correct strategy for everyone because mathematics has spoken.” — Avalanche usually costs the least interest when followed as modelled. But a plan also has to survive real habits, uneven months and low motivation. Choose with the cost difference and failure risk in plain view, not by slogan.

[heading] Model Rules: Make the Comparison Fair

[paragraph] The training model uses a fixed $560 monthly debt budget: $310 of modelled required payments plus $250 extra. Interest is added monthly at the yearly rate ÷ 12. Payments land at month-end, rates and model payments stay fixed, and final payments are capped. There are no new charges, fees, missed payments or penalties. Real statements and agreements will differ.

[cards]
  - Snowball order — $900 retail card → $1,500 family loan → $3,400 credit card → $8,000 line of credit
  - Avalanche order — 29.99% retail card → 24.99% credit card → 11.50% line of credit → 0% family loan

[myths]
  - “Snowball and avalanche always choose different first debts.” — Not necessarily. Devon’s $900 retail card is both the smallest balance and the highest rate, so both strategies target it first and clear it in model month four.

[spotlight] When one debt is both smallest and highest-rate, the first round is a tie. The real strategy difference begins with the next target.

[heading] Read the Scoreboard Without Declaring a Universal Champion

[ordered]
  - Avalanche wins the cost category. — The model pays off the higher-rate debt sooner, which lowers total interest.
  - Snowball wins the early-win category. — The 0% family loan is paid off earlier, so you have fewer open balances sooner.
  - Completion wins the real household category. — The plan you pick needs automatic payments, progress signals and a rule for tough months.

[myths]
  - “Snowball always gets a person debt-free faster because balances disappear sooner.” — Snowball may pay off accounts earlier, but it can cost more and may finish later. In Devon’s model it finishes one month later. Don’t confuse an early visible win with the final debt-free date.

[spotlight] Think of snowball’s extra cost as a motivation premium. Name that premium in dollars before you decide whether the earlier wins are worth it.

## Choose by Failure Risk

[carousel]
  - CHOOSE AVALANCHE WHEN — Interest savings matter most, and you can track progress without closing accounts. Your household is likely to follow a highest-rate order every month.
  - CHOOSE SNOWBALL WHEN — Early payoffs clearly help you stick with the plan, and you understand the extra projected cost. Smaller wins keep the plan from being dropped.
  - USE A DOCUMENTED SWITCH WHEN — The original strategy isn’t surviving real life, or a rate, balance, status or household condition has changed. Recalculate first. Don’t switch because a different statement looked dramatic that morning.

## Install the Roll-Forward Rule

[heading] The Roll-Forward Engine

[paragraph] When a target clears, keep the total monthly debt budget at $560 in the model.

[paragraph] The paid-off debt’s old payment joins the $250 extra and moves to the next target. If the freed payment quietly drifts back into spending, the tournament hasn’t rolled forward. It has wandered into the concession stand.

[spotlight] The strategy sets the order, and roll-forward speeds things up. Write down the next target before the current balance reaches zero.

[myths]
  - “Switching strategies means the repayment plan failed.” — A planned switch based on fresh numbers can make a plan stronger. Switching again and again without notes weakens focus. Record the reason, new order, projected trade-off and review date.

[heading] Province Lens: The Methods Do Not Change at the Border

[paragraph] Snowball and avalanche are household repayment strategies, not legal rights. The basic methods are the same in Alberta, British Columbia, New Brunswick, Ontario, Prince Edward Island, and Newfoundland and Labrador. Contracts, collection rules and formal debt remedies may differ, so check those separately.

[heading] Before You Run Your Own Repayment Tournament

[myths]
  - “A repayment forecast guarantees the exact interest and payoff month.” — A forecast is a planning model. Variable rates, daily interest, changing minimums, fees, new purchases and payment dates can all change the result. Compare the plan with your real statements every month.

[spotlight] Use identical assumptions for both routes: the same balances, rates, required payments, extra amount, timing and new-charge rule. Otherwise you are comparing two different games.

## Glossary of Terms

[glossary]
  - Debt snowball — A plan that sends extra money to the smallest balance while you keep making required payments on the other debts.
  - Debt avalanche — A plan that sends extra money to the highest interest rate while you keep making required payments on the other debts.
  - Target debt — The one balance that gets the planned extra payment.
  - Required payment — The minimum, scheduled or agreed amount you must pay under the account or plan.
  - Roll forward — Moving a paid-off debt’s old payment to the next target while keeping the total debt budget the same.
  - Interest efficiency — Paying down higher-rate debt sooner to cut the projected cost of borrowing.
  - Visible win — A clear sign of progress, such as paying off one balance.
  - Failure risk — The chance that a repayment plan gets dropped, keeps changing or becomes unaffordable.
  - Motivation premium — The extra projected cost you accept for earlier wins when snowball costs more than avalanche.
  - Repayment forecast — A planning estimate based on stated assumptions, not a promise of exact statement results.

8. VERDICT — Choose snowball, avalanche or a documented staged switch. State the trade-off, automation step, success measure and review date.
