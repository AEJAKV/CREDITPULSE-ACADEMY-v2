## Start here

[paragraph] A financial reset is not 30 days of plain toast while your banking app watches sternly from across the room.

[paragraph] It is a short expedition to see what is happening and stop preventable leaks. You protect the essentials and leave with a system that still works when Wednesday behaves like a Monday.

[paragraph] This final Money Foundations course turns the earlier lessons into one working routine. By Day 30, you will have a current budget, payday cash-flow map, fee cleanup, debt priority, starter emergency target, reset scorecard, 90-day goal and first monthly review date. You are building a useful system, not proving your worth through a spreadsheet.

[heading] EXPEDITION RULE

[paragraph] No shame, no financial crash diet and no demand for perfect attendance.

[paragraph] If you miss a task, resume at the next useful task. Restart the habit, not the calendar.

[spotlight] A reset succeeds when the system becomes easier to repeat, not when one unusually disciplined month wins an award.

[heading] Your Starting Reset Scorecard

[paragraph] Score each area 0, 1 or 2. This is a baseline, not a grade.

[paragraph] SELF-ASSESSMENT · 5 AREAS: 0 — Not visible; 1 — Partly working; 2 — Clear & repeatable.

[cards]

  - LEAK CONTROL — Fees and recurring charges are reviewed and intentional.
  - PROTECTION — Minimum payments and essentials have a reliable plan.
  - BUFFER — I have an emergency target and a repeatable transfer.

## Week 1—See Everything (Days 1–7)

[paragraph] Week 1 is observation, not self-prosecution.

[paragraph] Use recent pay records, bills and at least one to three months of account and credit statements when available. Estimates are allowed; label them and replace them later.

[carousel]
  - GATHER — Collect income records, bills, statements, debt notices and benefit information. Remove account numbers before sharing anything. [DAY 1]
  - INCOME — Record net income, benefit deposits and reliable side income. For variable income, separate the floor from the better-month amount. [DAY 2]
  - ESSENTIALS — List housing, utilities, food, transport, insurance, medication, care and required debt or support payments. [DAY 3]
  - FLEXIBLE SPENDING — Group ordinary choices such as dining, entertainment, shopping and convenience. Use honest categories, not moral labels. [DAY 4]
  - DEBTS — Record balance, rate, minimum, due date, arrears and security for every debt. Confirm current figures with the statement or lender. [DAY 5]
  - TIMING — Place paydays, deposits and due dates on one calendar. Mark the tightest gap and any bill that lands before the income meant to cover it. [DAY 6]
  - BASELINE — Build a current budget from what is happening now. Name the monthly surplus, shortfall or timing gap without trying to fix everything yet. [DAY 7]

[heading] WEEK 1 OUTPUT

[paragraph] One current budget + one payday cash-flow map + one complete debt list + the starting reset score.

[myths]
  - “I must track every cent before I can improve anything.” — Useful accuracy beats impossible perfection. Start with statements and reasonable estimates, mark missing information and improve the numbers during the month. Waiting for flawless data can become a well-organized form of delay.

[spotlight] A budget begins as a photograph of the current month; only then does it become a plan for the next one.

## Week 2—Stop the Leaks (Days 8–14)

[paragraph] A leak is a cost that no longer earns its place, a fee the system could prevent, or a bill that may be renegotiated. Do not cancel insurance, essential protection or contractual services without checking the consequences first.

[carousel]
  - FIND — Highlight bank fees, interest, NSF or overdraft costs, duplicate subscriptions, trials, unused memberships and service add-ons. [DAY 8]
  - SORT — Mark each item KEEP, CANCEL, PAUSE, DOWNGRADE or INVESTIGATE. Confirm cancellation dates and keep written proof. [DAY 9]
  - BANKING FIT — Compare the account’s fee, transaction limits and overdraft setup with actual use. Set a low-balance alert and ask about lower-cost options. [DAY 10]
  - NEGOTIATE — Contact one provider about a lower plan, hardship arrangement, corrected charge or due-date change. Record the name, date and result. [DAY 11]
  - REPAIR TIMING — Move eligible bill dates, create a small account cushion or change transfer timing. Never assume a date changed until confirmed in writing. [DAY 12]
  - REASSIGN — Send the first verified saving to one named job: the payday cushion, emergency fund, past-due bill or debt priority. [DAY 13]
  - VERIFY — Check that cancellations and changes took effect. A confirmation number is more useful than a hopeful screenshot. [DAY 14]

[source] FCAC: Low-cost and no-cost accounts
  url: https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/banking/bank-accounts/low-cost-no-cost.html

[myths]
  - “A small recurring fee is too minor to matter.” — One small charge may be harmless. Several forgotten charges, avoidable fees and financed add-ons can compete with a savings transfer every month. Judge each cost by annual total and actual value, not by how quietly it leaves.

[spotlight] Stopping a $20 monthly leak creates up to $240 of yearly room before interest or other effects, provided the saving is assigned a job.

## Weeks 3 and 4—Stabilize the System and Build the Next 90 Days

[heading] Week 3—Stabilize the System (Days 15–21)

[carousel]
  - PROTECT — Fund housing, food, utilities, transport, care and required minimums first; assign each bill to a payday. [DAYS 15–16]
  - BUFFER — Choose a starter emergency target and schedule a manageable transfer. Keep occasional costs in separate sinking funds. [DAYS 17–18]
  - PRIORITIZE — Bring past-due accounts current where possible, keep minimums on all debts, and send extra money to the chosen snowball or avalanche target. [DAYS 19–20]
  - TEST — Run a reduced-income or surprise-expense week. Adjust transfers before the plan requires borrowing for essentials. [DAY 21]

[heading] Week 4—Build the Next 90 Days (Days 22–30)

[carousel]
  - CHOOSE — Write one 90-day goal with a dollar amount, date and reason. Make it small enough to act on and important enough to keep. [DAYS 22–23]
  - MAP — Set three monthly milestones. Check federal and provincial benefits, consumer rights and relevant account options. [DAYS 24–25]
  - PROTECT — Confirm account alerts, payment reminders, deposit protection and a bad-month response: reduce, pause, contact or use the buffer. [DAYS 26–27]
  - SCHEDULE — Book the first monthly review. Put the date, time, records needed and a 20-minute limit on the calendar. [DAY 28]
  - RE-SCORE — Repeat the five-part reset scorecard. Name one improved area and one area that still needs support. [DAY 29]
  - DEPART — Confirm the next transfer, debt payment, bill date and 90-day milestone. Save the plan somewhere you will find it again. [DAY 30]

[myths]

[spotlight] The no-shame restart rule protects momentum: do the next useful task, recover the critical gap and keep the review date.

## Glossary of Terms

[glossary]
  - Financial reset — A short, structured review that improves visibility, timing, cost control, protection and next actions.
  - Baseline — The current financial picture before planned changes are counted as complete.
  - Cash-flow map — A calendar showing when income arrives and when bills, transfers and payments leave.
  - Timing gap — A shortage created when an obligation is due before the income intended to cover it arrives.
  - Money leak — A preventable fee or recurring cost that no longer provides enough value.
  - Sinking fund — Money saved gradually for a known but irregular future expense.
  - Emergency fund — Money set aside for unexpected expenses or income interruptions.
  - Debt priority — The account chosen to receive extra repayment after required minimums and urgent arrears are addressed.
  - Bad-month rule — A written order for adjusting goals and contacting providers when income falls or costs rise.
  - No-shame restart — Resuming with the next useful task instead of abandoning the plan or automatically returning to Day 1.

## Check-in

1. STARTING SNAPSHOT — List your monthly income, essential bills, everyday spending, debt payments, and the cash you have today. Add your starting score out of 10.
2. PAYDAY MAP — List each payday and the bills it must cover. Which date is the tightest, and what needs attention first?
3. LEAK CLEANUP — List three fees or charges you pay often. For each one, say Keep, Cancel, Pause, Downgrade, or Look Into It, and how much that saves in a year.
4. DEBT PRIORITY — List any overdue debts and your minimum payments. Pick one debt to pay down faster. How much extra can you send, and by when?
5. BUFFER PLAN — Set a small emergency savings goal, an amount to save each payday, and where it will go. Then name one expected cost, like winter tires, that deserves its own separate savings.
6. CANADA CHECKPOINT — Pick one fact to check on an official government or bank website: a benefit, your rights, an account fee, or how your deposits are protected.
7. NEXT 90 DAYS — Write one goal with a dollar amount and a date. Break it into three monthly steps. Which habit from this reset will make it happen?
8. REVIEW AND RESTART — Write your first review date, your Day 30 score, and your plan for a hard month. If you miss a few days, what will you do next?
